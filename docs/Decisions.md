| DEC13 | Water pace follows the time at the PC, not the clock: the amount expected by now is the goal times the time the user was at the PC (app running, session unlocked, PC awake) inside today's work window, divided by the window's length, rounded to 10 ml and capped at the goal. Locked, asleep, PC off and app not running add nothing, so after an unlock or a start the pace goes on from where it stopped. The 100 ml behind rule and the goal-reached pause are unchanged. Screen time (the same presence at any hour) is shown on its own: today's in the pet menu and keyboard panel, each day's in the weekly review and monthly progress | Owner request 28 October | After a long lock the app asked for a 600 ml backlog two minutes after the unlock, because the old pace spread the goal over the window by clock time. The time at the PC is kept with the day's tallies in the saved engine state, so it survives a restart the same day; each day's screen time is stored in `activity_day.screen_s` (migration 0007, additive, nullable: older rows are no data) because the reviews read up to a month of days |
| DEC14 | The memory budgets (V1 companion at most 150 MB; V2 companion and inference at most 350 MB during check-ins) count only the app's own memory: the private working set summed over the process tree, at the median and at the highest sample. A sample has a private value only when every process of the tree has a reading, and the readings must cover the samples (at least 80%, never more than 2 in a row missing) or the budget is not proven. The whole-tree working set is still recorded and reported, for information only. The limits themselves are unchanged | Owner decision 28 October 2026 | The 8-hour companion soak's whole-tree working set (median 218 MB, peak 335 MB, falling to about 97 MB late in the run) moved with pages shared with other programs, which Windows trims and re-adds at will; the app's own memory was steady (private working set median about 67 MB, private bytes flat). Under this rule that run passes the 150 MB budget. `Test-Soak.ps1` and `Test-CameraSoak.ps1` apply it |
| DEC15 | One combined 5-minute break replaces the separate movement, eye-rest and water reminders by default, also for existing users on upgrade. It is offered every 30 minutes of time at the PC (the DEC13 presence, inside work hours, not paused; Settings: 20, 30, 45 or 60) as one card, "Time for a 5-minute break", with Start, Snooze 10 min, Skip and Not now, under the same holds, calm gap, grace period and no-replay rules as any reminder. Start runs the steps in order, each skippable, with a countdown: drink water (10 s, "Log 250 ml" records a real water entry), an eye exercise (15 s), the guided movement routine (unchanged, still provisional), and slow breathing with the eyes closed for the rest of the 5 minutes (60 to 90 s, the meditation pause's opening). A timed step whose time runs out counts as done. Any step done restarts the break timer, and the eye-rest or movement timer when that step was done (all three for a finished break); none done (an immediate stop or every step skipped) restarts nothing, as for the routine. While it is on, water pace is still shown but raises no reminder, and the "fewer reminders" offer is not made (intensity applies to separate reminders only). The long-day check-in, Pomodoro, deadline-day recovery and V2 posture cues are unchanged, with one exercise at a time: starting a Pomodoro ends a running break (recorded as stopped), and while a break runs a paused Pomodoro cannot be resumed (the menu hides "Resume Pomodoro") and no Pomodoro round or break begins. Water logged outside the break's own button never ends it: from the menu or the water panel on the water step it completes that step (one entry) and the break goes on to the eyes; on a later step it is recorded and the break goes on; no confirmation card covers the break. The water panel or a phone note opened during a break shows over it, the step on screen waits, and closing that card (Cancel, close or its own time) returns to the break. The weekly review's "lighter reminders" suggestion, under the combined break, offers the next longer interval (20 to 30, 30 to 45, 45 to 60 minutes; none at 60) and says so, since intensity does not change the break. Settings > Reminders, "Reminder style": Combined 5-minute break (default) or Separate reminders, which restores the previous behaviour exactly; the movement and eye-rest timers keep counting meanwhile, so overdue reminders after a switch come one at a time with the calm gap between them, never as a burst. Stored as the additive `reminder_style` and `break_minutes` fields of the coaching settings | Owner request 2 November 2026, after using the app on an office PC | The separate reminders felt like too many notifications. For the records the smaller change was taken: the break's offer and answer are a movement reminder (`reminder.offered` and `reminder.response` with activity `movement`), so the weekly and monthly reviews count it as a break offered or taken and the shared outcomes count it as a reminder, with no schema or protocol change; the steps done go in a new `break.session` event (local only, no migration), the movement step writes its usual `movement.session`, and the water step its `water.logged` |
| DEC16 | Server-delivered companion updates (W1): the server administrator copies a release's two files (the installer and its `.sig`, unchanged) into a drop folder, `<server data folder>\updates`; there is no upload on the admin page. Paired, confirmed PCs read the offer over the pinned channel with their token (`GET /v1/update/manifest`, `/signature`, and `/installer` in pieces of at most 4 MiB), and the companion runs the file route's whole check (release key, name, version, rollback rule) before anything is staged. The server checks names and sizes only and is never trusted for authenticity; a refused file gets the file route's message | Built for the approved architecture 8 requirement (tracked as W1); open for Codex review | The admin page is a loopback, form-only page on the server PC, so its user can already copy files there; an upload would need a 200 MB request limit and a streaming upload path on the admin listener. The pieces keep every request inside the existing connection cap (2 minutes) and the client's 4 MiB response limit, so no limit was raised and no unauthenticated endpoint was added |
| DEC17 | Companion observability (W2, architecture 10). The log is `%LOCALAPPDATA%\HealthierYou\logs\companion.log`, JSON lines in the server's format, written only from typed values (counts, sizes, rounded measurements, booleans and names fixed in code); it is program state, so the uninstaller removes `logs\` while personal data stays. It rotates at 10 MB or when its first line is 7 days old; rotated files are kept 7 days and never more than 4. A panic logs only its source location (two path parts) and whether it was the window thread, never its message. A release build ends the process on a panic (`panic = "abort"`, workspace-wide), so the hook starts the app again, at most once in 10 minutes, and the new process waits for the old one to end. A pet page whose WebView2 renderer or browser process failed is destroyed and made again, at most 5 times in 10 minutes; another window whose page failed is closed, and the camera page is left to the camera's watchdog. The diagnostics zip is built in memory from every log file through a second redaction pass (paths, e-mail addresses, long ids and the PC's own names replaced; lines that are not log lines dropped), holds the newest 2 MB of log lines and an `about.txt`, is shown to the user file by file in full, and only the shown bytes are written, where the user chooses. It is a stored (uncompressed) zip written without a new dependency. Nothing is uploaded | Built for tracked work W2, 2 November 2026 | Architecture 10 names the four parts but not their limits. These keep the log's disk use bounded (about 50 MB at most), make a fault at start-up unable to loop, keep the zip small enough to read in full before saving, and add no crate to the supply chain (W3) |
